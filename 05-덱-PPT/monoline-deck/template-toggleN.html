Three levels of concept: policy ⊃ social policy ⊃ social welfare policy

Policy every goal-oriented course of action of a public agent
Social policy policies that directly affect citizens’ welfare
Social welfare policy income and services to meet social needs
Policy is the highest category: every goal-oriented course of action of a public agent
Social policy, contrasted with economic policy, covers welfare, health, education, housing
Social welfare policy guarantees income and services to meet social needs
The inclusion is not strict — some scholars treat social policy and social welfare policy as synonyms
Chapter 2 refines the comparison with related concepts

Residual or institutional? — Wilensky & Lebeaux

Residual
a safety valve when family and market fail
vs
Institutional
a normal, first-line institution for all citizens
Residual: welfare operates only when the “normal” channels — family and market — fail
Receiving help is exceptional and transitory; means tests and stigma follow easily
Institutional: welfare is a normal, permanent function of industrial society
A first-line institution every citizen uses at some phase of life — welfare as a right
Is the Basic Pension residual or institutional? Both — that ambiguity returns later
